Luncher is made with performance in mind, to run smoothly even on old and weak Android TV devices, and still gives you plenty of customization options to shape it to your taste.

Features:

- A status bar with the time, the date and the settings, which you can arrange to your liking
- Your TV apps as a grid of banners, placed where you want on the screen
- Reorder your apps
- Hide the apps you don't use, from a tile's menu (a long press of OK), and show them again in the settings
- Customize the tile shape, from square to pill-shaped
- Lift the focused tile with a soft shadow instead of a frame, and show its app's name below it
- Set your own pictures as wallpaper, with dim and blur options (shared from any app or, up to Android 14, copied from a USB drive)
- Pick an accent color, and dark or light menus and settings

On the way:

- Your own custom banners for app tiles
- More appearance settings: tile size and spacing and much more
- Wi-Fi, VPN and notifications in the status bar, without ever asking for internet access
- Folders to group your apps

Application goals:

- Instant startup and minimal memory usage, with no background services
- Offline by design: no internet, no ads, no tracking, no accounts
- No frameworks or dependencies, just plain Kotlin

Once installed, Luncher shows up in your TV's list of apps. You can open it from there to try it out, but the Home button on the remote still takes you back to the TV's own launcher. Detailed instructions to make it your default home screen depend on the device model.
